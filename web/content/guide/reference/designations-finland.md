Finnish, like German, joins words together to describe equipment precisely, so military terms get long and are almost always shortened. On top of that, Finland has its own way of coding aircraft and armoured vehicles, which is where markings like "R-210" or "Ps. 221-6" come from. This page explains the abbreviations and numbering you'll meet on Finnish vehicles in War Thunder.

## Air Force aircraft codes

Finland's first military aircraft was a Thulin Typ D, given by the Swedish Count Eric von Rosen and designated **F.1** in 1918 (F for Flygmaskin, Swedish for "aircraft"). As the air force grew, the system was rewritten several times to add information such as role, type, origin and year of purchase.

The fifth version, brought in in 1927, is still used today and is pleasingly simple:

- Each aircraft type gets a **two-letter code**, taken from the maker's name, the original designation or a nickname.
- Each airframe then gets a **sequential number**.

An extra lower-case letter sometimes marks a sub-type, often the engine fitted.

## The armour registry

Finnish armoured vehicles are listed in the Panssarirekisteri (armour registry), formally *Suomen puolustusvoimien panssariajoneuvojen rekisterimerkinnät*. It began with a numbering system prefixed R, which was later replaced by the Ps system still in use.

### Before the R numbers

Before the Winter War there were no standard markings. The Renault F.T. tanks kept their original French registration numbers (numéro de matricule), painted on the glacis or the lower suspension frame. They also had a Finnish serial number, in the French style, painted in a white square on the inside of the driver's left hatch, so it could only be seen with the hatch open. Armoured cars captured in the Civil War carried improvised markings much like those on military cars.

### R numbers

The first standard registration numbers came with the Vickers 6-ton tanks: a three-digit number in white on a plate on the left rear mudguard. During the Winter War the scheme was extended to captured tanks and changed to a running number of one to four digits, painted in white 100 mm digits on the front and rear of the hull. Official documents put an "R" (Rekisterinumero) in front, as in R-210, but only the digits were painted on the vehicle.

R numbers stayed in use until the Ps code was adopted in 1943. The last R-registered vehicles didn't get Ps codes until 1945, after the war, when they were repaired for peacetime service.

The R system was never orderly or fully documented. Numbers were handed out as vehicles were captured, leaving gaps, overlaps and inconsistencies, and much of what is known has been pieced together from photos and surviving vehicles. Some numbers may never have been used, some were reassigned, and some blocks mixed quite different vehicles.

A few oddities stand out:

- **R-0** went to the Landsverk 182 armoured car.
- **R-96 to R-100** were used twice: first for BT-5 and BT-7 tanks, later for T-26 and KV-1 tanks.
- **T-20 Komsomolets tractors** were numbered upwards from R-400. When R-499 was reached, numbering carried on backwards from R-399 down to R-321.

### Ps. codes (1943 onwards)

In summer 1943 the R system was replaced by the more structured Ps. registration (from Panssarivaunu, "armoured vehicle"). A Ps. code has three parts:

1. the prefix **Ps.**,
2. a **model number** of one to three digits, given out in blocks by vehicle type, and
3. a hyphen and the **individual vehicle number**.

So **Ps. 221-6** is Pz.IV number 6. The full stop after "Ps" was usually painted on during and just after the war, but dropped in the late 1950s when the Soviet-supplied vehicles were repainted.

| Ps. block | Vehicle type |
|---|---|
| 1–99 | Armoured cars |
| 101–349 | Battle tanks (light, medium and main battle tanks) |
| 351–399 | Amphibious tanks |
| 401–449 | Flamethrower tanks |
| 451–499 | Anti-aircraft vehicles |
| 501–549 | Assault guns |
| 551–599 | Command vehicles |
| 601–649 | Training tanks |
| 651–699 | Transporters |
| 701–749 | Recovery vehicles |
| 751–799 | Armoured tractors |

The register grew over the following decades:

- **1959:** categories for bridge layers, mine-clearing tanks and anti-tank vehicles were added, though none of these were ever actually fielded.
- **From 1967:** starting with the BTR-50PU command vehicles, some vehicles were given type codes that didn't fit the system.
- **1978:** a Ps. 800 block was created for unarmoured tracked artillery tractors and tank transporters, which had previously been on the SA automotive register.
- **Early 1990s:** modern tanks no longer fitted the old structure, so the register was overhauled completely. The new system was ordered in February 1992.
