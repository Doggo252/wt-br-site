Night vision devices (NVDs) arrived with Update 1.91 "Night Vision" for a number of high-tier tanks and helicopters, at the same time as night battles became more common. There are two kinds, image intensifiers and thermal imagers. Tanks that had them historically also got infrared (IR) searchlights.

## Getting and using them

- NVDs are normally tier 3–4 modifications, though some vehicles get them at tier 1–2.
- On some tanks the image intensifier can be upgraded to a thermal imager with a second modification. On others the thermal comes as part of the first one.
- Different crew positions can have different devices. A typical setup gives the gunner a thermal sight, the commander (or a helicopter's pilot) an image intensifier, and the driver a weaker intensifier.
- Each device only works from its own view: the commander's through the binoculars, the driver's from the driver's seat or third person, and the gunner's from the gunner sight.

The relevant controls are **Night Vision Mode** (switches between normal view and intensifier or thermal view), **IR Spotlight** (switches the IR searchlight on and off) and the thermal colour scheme control (switches between White Hot and Black Hot). Check your control settings for their keys.

## Image intensifiers

An image intensifier boosts the light coming in and turns some infrared light into visible light. Switch one on and the view turns green and you can see much further. It **only works at night**.

Intensifiers suffer from noise, a TV-static effect over the picture. It is strong on early devices and much weaker on advanced ones, which usually means newer tanks get a clearer picture.

Three things set one intensifier apart from another, and newer devices tend to be better at all three:

- **Resolution.** Tank intensifiers are usually 800 x 600 or 1600 x 1200. The difference is small and mostly shows at long range: a tank about 1.25 km away looks a little sharper, and its barrel becomes visible.
- **Light multiplier.** This is how much the device amplifies light. A higher multiplier gives a brighter, more detailed picture. The spread between the best and worst on tanks isn't huge, but it shows, especially under an IR searchlight. The T-80U's gunner sight (9.0) shows clearly more grass and bush detail than its driver's periscope (5.0).
- **Noise.** Less static means a cleaner image.

## IR searchlights

An IR searchlight works like an ordinary searchlight, but its light is infrared and invisible to the naked eye. An image intensifier can see it, though, so with your intensifier on, whatever the beam points at is lit up brightly.

**The catch:** every enemy using an intensifier can see your beam too. Players on the ground see the lit-up area and the lamp itself as a bright spot. A helicopter sees the lit area, and because the beam spreads out, it forms a triangle pointing back at you. Switching it on gives your position away.

Searchlights differ in **maximum range** (how far they can still light something up) and **beamwidth** (how wide the beam is, in degrees). Some are slightly brighter than others, but the differences are too small to matter at present.

## Thermal imagers

Thermal imagers are a step up. They pick up the heat objects give off and turn it into a picture, so anything warmer than its surroundings stands out. In the game they are usually fitted to the gunner's sight only. They **work by day as well as at night** and make it easy to spot targets at very long range. A tank hidden in bushes shows up clearly, and engine decks glow hot.

There are two display modes:

- **White hot:** terrain is dark grey, and heat sources (vehicles, fires, projectiles and so on) show from light grey to bright white depending on temperature.
- **Black hot:** terrain is light grey, and heat sources show from dark grey to black.

What really separates one thermal sight from another is resolution. Most are fairly low-resolution and blurry. The lowest in the game is 500 x 300 and the highest 1200 x 800. The higher-resolution sights give noticeably more detail, especially at range.

### Thermal colour options

Ground vehicles can also change the thermal palette under Options, then Main parameters, then Ground vehicle battle settings, then "color scheme of the thermal sight". The choices are grey (default), green and red, and the choice applies in both normal and inverted modes. It doesn't change what the sight can see, but some players spot targets more easily in one colour than another, so try each.
