Large bluewater ships that carry a catapult can launch a small scout seaplane in naval battles. Seaplanes had been in the game before, but only as ordinary tech tree aircraft flown in air battles; the "Winged Lions" update turned them into a tool for the ship itself. A scout plane can look far ahead, sit on a capture zone, lay smoke and harass enemy ships with whatever it carries.

## Spotting

The first thing you notice after launch is how much you can see. Enemy ships across most of the map show up from the air, which gives you a far better picture of the battle. Your ship's own spotting doesn't change, though.

The plane is also an excellent torpedo lookout. From altitude, torpedo wakes stand out kilometres away, giving you time to steer around them while your guns stay on target.

## Capturing zones

Capturing points is one of the scout plane's main jobs. On big high-rank maps even the fastest bluewater ships can take minutes to reach a zone, while the plane gets there quickly. Later in the match, if an enemy-held zone has no hostile ships nearby, it's worth trying to take it with the plane.

To land on a zone:

1. Fly in at full speed.
2. Cut the throttle to zero.
3. Bleed off speed with hard turns and rudder (Q and E on PC by default) until your indicated airspeed is 200 km/h or lower. Watch out for stalling.
4. Set down gently.

To keep moving on the water so you're harder to hit, set the throttle to around 55% and adjust from there.

## Smoke screens

Smoke is one of the most common uses. The plane's advantage over the ship's own Smokescreen modification is placement: it can drop the screen right across the line between you and the enemy, so the cover works better and gives you longer to get away. If someone keeps chasing you, alternate: lay smoke from the plane while the ship's smoke is reloading, and you can stay covered almost continuously.

The plane can hide itself too. Approach the edge of the capture zone, start laying smoke just as you begin capturing, then turn in towards the middle so you sit behind your own screen.

## Attacking and defending

A scout plane with bombs can go after enemy ships to protect your own. It's very risky and usually ends badly: these planes are fragile, and a single 20 mm hit is often enough to bring one down. Still, a couple of small bombs into a destroyer's ammunition can change a battle. Use mountains, icebergs and other hard cover on the way in, because within 500 m of a ship, even the best pilots will struggle to escape untouched.

The more reliable job is hunting other aircraft: enemy scout planes, bombers and even fighters. The Ar 196 A-3 suits this well, since its 20 mm cannons do damage quickly. Planes with smaller guns take longer to cause serious damage, which is riskier if the target shoots back. Rear guns are very unreliable against aircraft, but aimed by hand they can still catch an enemy scout plane that isn't paying attention.

## Rearming

Arcade refills a plane's guns and bombs in the air, but Realistic doesn't, and the plane can't return to its ship or an airfield either. That leaves two ways to reload bombs and ammunition:

- land on a naval landing strip, or
- land on a capture point, which rearms you much like a tank in ground battles.
