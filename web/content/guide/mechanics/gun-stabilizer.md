---
title: Gun Stabilizer
summary: The three kinds of tank gun stabilizer (shoulder-stop, single-plane and two-plane) and how well each lets you shoot on the move.
source: https://wiki.warthunder.com/mechanics/199-gun-stabilizer
---

A gun stabilizer holds the gun on your aim point while the hull pitches and bounces underneath it. Without one, you have to stop and wait for the gun to settle before you can take an accurate shot. With one, you can fire on the move, or the moment you stop. In a tank duel those saved seconds often decide who shoots first.

There are three types in the game.

| Type | What it steadies | Works while moving up to |
|---|---|---|
| Shoulder-stop | Gun elevation, held by the gunner | about 8–9 km/h |
| Single-plane | Vertical axis | a speed limit set for each tank (on most tanks) |
| Two-plane | Vertical and horizontal axes | any speed |

## Shoulder-stop

This isn't a real stabilizer. It reflects how some small guns, such as the 2-pounder, were aimed: the gunner raised and lowered the gun with his shoulder against a rest instead of turning a geared handwheel, so he could keep it fairly steady while the tank moved. In the game it behaves like a rougher version of the single-plane type and switches off above about 8–9 km/h.

## Single-plane

This stabilizes the vertical axis only. Driving fast across rough ground, your sight will still bounce around a fair amount, but the gun settles quickly when you stop to fire.

On most tanks it only works up to a certain speed. Go faster and it switches off, and the gun bounces with the terrain until you slow down again.

## Two-plane

The best type, stabilizing both the vertical and the horizontal axis. The sight stays smooth even cross-country, and it works at any speed.

One catch: after you've driven over rough, hilly ground, the sight can be back on the target while the gun itself is still out of line for a few seconds. Give it a moment to catch up before you fire.

## Using it

- With shoulder-stop or single-plane, slow down below the cut-off speed before you take a moving shot, or stop briefly.
- With two-plane, you can fire on the move freely. Just mind the short lag after rough terrain.
