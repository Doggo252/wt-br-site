Drones are aircraft flown by remote control, with nobody on board: UAVs (unmanned aerial vehicles) and UCAVs (unmanned combat aerial vehicles). Update "Drone Age" brought them into ground battles in two classes, **scout drones** and **strike drones**.

## Scout drones

Scout drones are tiny and unarmed. Their only job is to watch the battlefield for you.

- **Who gets them:** every light tank of rank VI and above, as a modification.
- **Thermal or not:** drones from rank VI light tanks have no thermal vision, and those from rank VII light tanks do.
- **Flying one:** launch it whenever you like during a battle, then switch between drone and tank as needed. If you set the tank on cruise control, it keeps driving while you fly.
- **Replacing a lost drone:** in Realistic, restock on a captured point. In Arcade, a new one arrives by itself after about 3 minutes.
- **Model:** the ZALA 421-16EM.

### In Realistic

The drone doesn't spot enemies for your team by itself. Whatever it sees, you have to pass on, so it's at its best in a squad with voice chat. Without voice, use the "Set target for squad" control to drop a precise marker on an enemy's position for your squad. It has no cooldown. With no squad at all, "Designate a team target" marks the spot on the map for the whole team, with the same marker and a distinctive UAV buzz.

### In Arcade

Here the drone counts as a friendly aircraft watching the battle. Every enemy it sees is shown on the minimap through the "radio communication" skill, as long as your allies are close enough to those enemies. That beats waiting for an allied airstrike to scout for you: there's no delay, and no enemy fighter can spot you on its map. It's ideal before crossing a ridge or when rooting out campers. It also warns teammates who never read chat, taking away the surprise of enemies up to 1 km from them. You can mark points of interest as in Realistic too.

Some cautions for Arcade:

- The spotting only lasts while you are actually flying the drone.
- Everyone can see the drone. It's very hard to hit, but just being in the air can set off SPAA and tanks firing proximity-fused shells.
- Shooting a drone down earns little SL and RP, but it does earn an **airstrike point**. Enemy SAM vehicles, light tanks and SPGs with HE-VT shells can farm drones to pay for bombers, or even a nuke if your team launches drones nonstop. Don't fly straight at enemy tanks, and launch the drone when you need information, not for the sake of it.

### Orbit and the camera

The easiest way to scout is to fly over the area you're interested in and order **"Flying in orbit"**. The drone then circles anticlockwise at its current height, on a circle about 400 m across that sits to the left of where you gave the order, taking about 45 seconds a lap, until you cancel. You can then switch to the TV camera underneath and zoom in. Sight stabilization is available, and you can lock the camera onto a point. The drone remembers that point every time you switch back, until you move the camera or pick a new spot. Set it orbiting, go back to your tank, and check in when you need to.

## Strike drones

Strike drones are larger and carry air-to-surface guided missiles. You can spawn one in a ground battle once you have 750 spawn points, plus extra depending on the payload. The MQ-1 is one example.

- They carry up to two missiles, then have to fly back to an airfield to rearm.
- They spawn high up and are smaller than manned aircraft, but they're slow and clumsy. Any fighter or SPAA that spots one can bring it down easily.

## Where drones came from

The scout drone first appeared in the 2021 April Fools event "Warfare 2077", launched from the event's "Hydra" rocket tank. That version also had a shaped-charge warhead, so it could be used as a loitering munition against armour. The current scout drone uses the same model but is for reconnaissance only.
