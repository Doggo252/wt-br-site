A mortar is a smoothbore weapon that only fires steeply upwards, dropping its projectile onto the target from above. It is built for indirect fire, at things you often can't see directly. Its projectiles are properly called bombs. Mortars tend to be small and light, with less range than other guns.

## How mortars compare

| Compared with | How a mortar differs |
|---|---|
| Howitzers | Shorter range, no shallow-angle fire, usually lighter, and a smoothbore barrel instead of a rifled one |
| Cannons | Indirect fire only, no shallow-angle fire, less range, and a long flight time that makes moving targets hard to hit |
| Rockets | The bomb has no motor of its own. A propellant charge burns in the tube and the gas pressure throws the bomb out. |

The exception is the rocket mortar, common in the Second World War. It fired rockets as its ammunition, could shoot at flatter angles, and sometimes had a rifled barrel, breaking every one of the usual rules.

## Mortars in the game

- **Naval:** mortars came with the first naval forces in Update 1.83 "Masters of the Sea". They included the Y-gun Mk.VII depth charge and mortar-launched WBG depth charges.
- **Ground:** the first ground vehicle with a mortar was the Matilda Hedgehog, added in Update "New Power".

## Using them

The big advantage is landing a large explosive charge on a target that's out of your line of sight. The high arc brings plenty of problems with it:

- The target has to be in just the right place.
- Moving targets need a lot of lead.
- Mortars have spread, so some luck is involved, and a target that keeps moving can easily dodge.

Most mortars have just one kind of ammunition, usually impact-fused high-explosive or a depth charge.

### Naval aiming

There are no aiming aids at all. The "Highlight projectile fall point", "Highlight targeting point" and "Play buzzer sound when projectile hits the water" settings don't apply to mortars, and no interface element helps you aim.

### Ground aiming

It depends on the vehicle:

- **Matilda Hedgehog:** a text readout tells you how far away the rockets will land (for example "Hedgehog Rockets - 220m"). There are no other aids.
- **Churchill AVRE Petard:** you set the impact point with the "Sight distance control" keys. Setting those up is covered under zeroing in [Optics](/guide/mechanics/optics/).
