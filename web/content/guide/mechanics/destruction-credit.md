---
title: Destruction Credit
summary: Who gets the kill and who gets the assist for aircraft, ground vehicles and ships, with each damage type and how long it counts.

---

When several players hit the same target, the game has to decide who gets the kill and who gets an assist. Each vehicle class has its own rules for this, built so the reward is split as fairly and predictably as possible between everyone who took part. The rules for a vehicle class are the same in every game mode; only some of the timers change.

## Aircraft

The kill, and the biggest reward, goes to the player who destroys the aircraft.

**When a kill counts:**

- **Instantly,** if your damage makes controlled flight impossible: the pilot is killed, a fuel tank explodes, the middle or inner section of a wing comes off, the tail comes off, and so on.
- **Otherwise,** you get the kill if your damage helped bring the aircraft down while your damage timer was still running, and no other condition applied.

**Assists:** every player who did critical or serious damage to the aircraft before their timer ran out gets the secondary (support) award.

**Bailing out** counts as being shot down. The kill goes to the nearest enemy within 5 km, unless there is still active damage on the aircraft that can be credited to someone else.

**Emergency landing** at an airfield where the aircraft can be repaired wipes the recorded damage, and no kill is awarded.

### Damage types

From most to least severe: critical, then serious, then simple.

| Damage type | Timer | What counts |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | 90 seconds, after which it drops to the lowest priority | Anything that seriously harms handling or performance, from a failed rudder to fuselage damage |
| Serious | 30 / 60 / 80 seconds (AB / RB / SB), then reset | Significant damage that doesn't seriously harm performance, including leaks of oil, water or fuel |
| Simple | 12 seconds in AB, 15 seconds in RB and SB, then reset | Any other hit |

How each one works:

- **Critical:** if the aircraft goes down with several critical hits on it, the player who did the heaviest one gets the kill. Some critical damage acts slowly. A damaged radiator, for example, overheats the engine until it fails, and the critical damage is then credited to whoever hit the radiator. This delayed damage has no timer and stays assigned until the aircraft is destroyed or repaired. A fire, by contrast, counts as normal damage with a timer. Once a critical damage timer runs out, it is still assigned to the target but ranks below any serious or simple damage whose timer is still running. Critical damage always pays Silver Lions and Research Points, whatever happens to the target afterwards, and shows in red in the HUD and battle log.
- **Serious:** shown in grey in the HUD and battle log.
- **Simple:** once its timer runs out, it no longer earns the support award.

### Crashes without damage

Planes and helicopters that crash without taking any damage still pay out. The reward goes to the nearest enemy player within 3 km of the crash. If nobody is that close, it goes to the player whose guided missile was nearest the aircraft, again within 3 km. The payout is 50% of the normal amount, and it's switched off in Air Simulator battles.

## Ground vehicles

The kill, and the biggest reward, goes to the player who destroys the vehicle.

**When a kill counts:**

- **Instantly,** if the vehicle explodes, or if it no longer has enough crew to drive and shoot at the same time.
- **Otherwise,** you are paid for causing any of the damage types below, and you get the kill when your damage is what finishes the vehicle.

**Assists:** every player whose serious or critical damage still has an active timer when the vehicle is destroyed gets the secondary (support) award.

**Bailing out** destroys the vehicle. The kill goes to a player who did one of these, checked in this order: active fire, then simple damage, then critical damage, then serious damage.

**Repairs don't clear damage.** Fixing a vehicle makes it fit to fight again, but the damage already recorded against it stays active.

### Damage types

From most to least severe: active fire, critical, serious, simple.

| Damage type | Timer | What counts |
|---|---|---|
| Active fire | No timer of its own; it sets the timer for the fire's damage | A hit that sets the fuel tank or engine on fire |
| Critical | No timer | Knocking out a crew member, a weapon, the engine or the transmission |
| Serious | 30 / 60 / 80 seconds (AB / RB / SB), then reset | Failure of the engine, transmission, barrel or breech |
| Simple | 12 / 15 / 15 seconds (AB / RB / SB), then reset | Any hit that does damage |

How each one works:

- **Active fire:** the fire can go on to cause both simple and critical damage, and all of it ends up credited to the player who started it.
- **Critical:** the only damage type that still earns the Assist in destruction award after time has passed. It pays Silver Lions and Research Points once per target and is recorded in the battle log.
- **Serious and simple:** either can be what destroys the vehicle. For example, if a shell detonates in a damaged barrel, the vehicle counts as destroyed.

## Ships

For ships, the biggest reward goes to whoever did the damage. A ship can carry thousands of sailors, so sinking one in a single shot only happens in exceptional cases. Instead, the game measures your contribution by how much of the crew you knock out.

**When a kill counts:**

- **Instantly,** if you detonate the ship's magazine (ammo rack), destroy its hull, or destroy every compartment. The ship and its whole crew are credited to you.
- **Otherwise,** rewards are shared by the percentage of the ship's survivability crew each player took out.

**Assists:** any player who removed at least 20% of the destroyed ship's survivability crew gets the secondary (assist) award.

### How crew loss works

A ship's crew is split between compartments and stations: weapons, sensors, the captain's cabin and so on. Hitting a compartment or station knocks out some of the crew assigned to it, depending on how bad the hit is.

- A compartment shown in black has been destroyed, and none of its crew are left.
- Crew are packed more densely into compartments that haven't been hit, and fires keep causing further damage.
- The damage camera shows how many crew remain, both as a number and as a percentage.
