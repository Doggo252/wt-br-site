An autoloader is a machine that loads the gun and gets rid of spent cases so no crewman has to. Tanks, infantry fighting vehicles, self-propelled guns and even anti-aircraft guns use them. In real vehicles the aim is to make the crew's job easier, shrink the crew and, in most cases, fire faster. In the game it does the same job.

## What it changes in battle

- **A fixed reload.** The main gun's reload becomes a constant value that does not depend on a loader's skill or condition.
- **Fires don't cost you the reload.** Extinguishing a fire mid-reload doesn't throw away the progress already made.
- **It may not reach all your ammo.** Often the autoloader can only use its own limited supply, the first-stage ammunition. When that is empty you have to wait for it to be refilled; see [first-stage ammo stowage](/guide/mechanics/first-stage-ammo-stowage/) for how that works.
- **It can be knocked out.** Since Update 2.39 "Dance of Dragons" the autoloader is a proper module, and when it is damaged it stops working. A round already in the gun can still be fired, but anything after that has to wait for repairs. See [ground vehicle modules](/guide/mechanics/ground-vehicle-modules/) for the other parts that can fail.

## The three types

The game has three styles of autoloader. They differ only in how they look, not in how they behave.

| Type | What it looks like | Where you usually find it | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Belt | A loading system built directly into the turret | The most common type, mostly on Western-built tanks | KPz-70, Type 10, Leclerc SXXI, Object 906 |
| Carousel | Rounds laid out in a ring on the floor of the fighting compartment | The most common type on Soviet main battle tanks | T-80BVM, T-90M, ZTZ99A |
| Revolver | A cylindrical drum | The most common type on French tanks with oscillating turrets | Lorraine 40T, AMX-13, AMX-50 (TOA100), T54E1 |

## A short history

The first large-scale users were the French AMX-13 in the 1950s and the Austrian Steyr SK 105 in 1971. Both used revolver loaders with two drums of six shells each, and the drums could only be refilled away from the fighting. Their oscillating turrets made the mechanism much simpler to design.

In 1969 AAI Corp. built an experimental, fully automatic system that could fire up to eight rounds per minute, load while the gun was being aimed in both directions, and work together with a gun stabiliser. The Soviet T-64 had one of the earliest true tank autoloaders, and others followed in production tanks such as the French Leclerc and Japanese Type 90, and in prototypes such as the American MBT-70.

Dropping the loader takes a typical tank crew from four (commander, driver, gunner, loader) down to three, which lets designers make the tank lighter, smaller and cheaper. The USSR committed to three-man crews in 1958. By the early 2000s the two big tank-building nations still disagreed: Russian designers favoured autoloaders for the weight saving, while American designers preferred a human loader for a higher initial rate of fire and a lighter workload per crewman. The weight saving did win over American light tank designers, though. On the XM8, an autoloader holding 21 rounds took up the same space as a loader with only nine.
