Optics are the sights and vision devices your crew looks through: the gunner's sight, the commander's binoculars from the cupola, and on many tanks a separate commander's sight. Their zoom and quality decide how easily you can find a target and put a shell on its weak spot.

## Gunner sight

You enter it with "sniper aim" (Shift on a keyboard by default). It opens at minimum zoom. Switch between minimum and maximum zoom with the mouse wheel, the "zoom in" key (Z by default) or any key you bind for it. Every gunner sight has a crosshair and a compass, and most have range marks for the ammunition you have loaded.

## Commander sight

From Rank V upwards, most tanks (not all) have a commander sight. Bind the "commander scope sight" key first, because it is unbound by default. Zoom works as in the gunner sight. It has no proper range marks, so at first it is mainly for scouting and rangefinding.

Better-equipped tanks go further: bind "use commander sight to aim" and you can fire from it. "Aim mode" then shows in the top-left corner, and in Arcade you also get the gunner's range estimate. The setting sticks: from then on the commander takes over the weapons whenever you use his sight, until you press the key again. On these tanks:

- If the gunner is knocked out, the commander quickly takes over firing.
- On most (not all) tanks that fire ATGMs and let the commander take the weapons, using the commander sight moves missile guidance onto the commander's optics for as long as you stay in them, instead of the gun (in AB and RB) or the gunner's sight (in SB). That matters for gun-launched missiles: the gun view shakes badly on the move and can steer a missile into the ground, while the commander sight stays steady.
- If the commander is knocked out, his sight and all these advantages are gone.

The commander's sight can have a different zoom and field of view from the gunner's. It's usually not stronger, with some exceptions. On older tanks the commander only gets ordinary night vision, while most modern tanks give him a thermal sight like the gunner's. The NVD or thermal modification itself lists which crew members get which night vision (see [Night Vision Devices](/guide/mechanics/night-vision/)).

**Binoculars** are always about 6x, with the same lenses on every vehicle.

## Driver view

Bind "driver camera" to sit in the driver's seat. It's handy for checking whether you're properly hull-down. In rare cases, when smoke has blinded the gunner and commander and third person shows nothing, it can give you some sight of what's right around you. While you're in it, every control except driving is disabled.

## Checking optics in the hangar

In X-ray view, optics are marked on the vehicle. Hover over one for an "optic" pop-up, and some pop-ups list zoom, field of view and other details. On vehicles with several sets of optics, usually only one (often the gunner's) shows the stats, but not always, so check them all. Optics can also be buried among other modules, as on the AMX-30, so you may need to rotate the model to find them.

- If only one zoom figure is listed, treat it as the maximum. The Ho-Ri Production shows only "Zoom: 6.0x", and its field of view later turned out to be 12°.
- Pop-ups can be incomplete or change, so test for yourself. For vehicles with no zoom listed, take them into a test drive and compare a fixed object against vehicles whose zoom you know.
- Listed field of view ignores vignetting, the dark edge some older sights have. It simply follows the zoom level: a 10x sight almost always says 7°, even when the outer 2° are hidden in the dark.
- Tanks that can track aircraft with their optics (IRST) but have no separate camera for it describe the tracker in the gunner's optics pop-up. It isn't always listed, so check manually past rank VI.
- If the commander can control the main armament, his pop-up usually says so, though not always.

## What zoom tells you

- Brawlers built for close fighting rarely go above 4x.
- Tanks with 12x or more are built to snipe from further back.
- In Arcade, more zoom makes it easier to put a quick shot on a small weak spot, and it spreads out the shell drop marks so you can judge your angle more easily.

Experienced players can stretch either type: make long shots with weak optics, or brawl with a sniper.

## Optics as weak spots

Vision ports are weak points in the armour. Even a super-heavy like the IS-7 has them, although they are tiny and hard to hit. On some light SPGs and tanks, the optics often have less than 10 mm of RHA behind them. A shell with a lot of explosive filler can kill the tank through overpressure.

When optics are damaged, they can't be used until they are repaired. That doesn't usually matter beyond losing the view, but some vehicles depend on them. The Warrior, for instance, can't launch ATGMs if its commander's optics are destroyed.

## Zeroing your gun

Some sights just don't suit their gun. A 16x sight on a low-velocity demolition gun crams the range marks together, while weak zoom and poor lenses on a high-velocity APDS or APFSDS gun spread them out. With early HEAT shells the marks can make the sight useless, and rocket tanks have no marks at all. The fix is to zero the gun to a set distance and then aim straight at the target.

**Setting it up.** Go to Controls → Tank → Miscellaneous → Sight distance control, turn on "relative control", and bind "increase value" and "decrease value". Increase raises the gun to shoot further, and decrease brings it back down.

**Finding the distance.**

- In Realistic, use the "rangefinding" key (unbound by default, under Controls → Tank → Miscellaneous next to the sight controls), or estimate it. A rangefinder modification and a high Rangefinding crew skill make it faster, more accurate and longer-ranged.
- In Arcade, the gunner always tells you the exact distance to whatever is under your crosshair. Zero to that number and, with the accuracy upgrades researched, you'll almost always hit. That can be easier than reading the marks: 10x APFSDS sights, for example, have no number at 1.6 km, only a large mark.
- Some tanks have poor sights but a quick rangefinder. The AMX-50 Foch is one, and this method suits it well.

**Watch out for:**

- Zeroing is set for the shell you have loaded. Switch to a shell with a different velocity and the same setting means a different distance: zeroed for HE at 450 m, an APFSDS round might be sent out to 2,100 m. Bind a reset key too, especially on APDS tanks.
- Low-velocity chemical rounds (550 m/s and below) drop twice as steeply past 1,000 m, sometimes from 800 m, so even a zeroed sight may not show where they'll land.
- Don't make the adjustment too sensitive or too sluggish.
- A zeroed gun aims at the set distance, so an enemy who gets closer than that needs you to aim **below** him.
