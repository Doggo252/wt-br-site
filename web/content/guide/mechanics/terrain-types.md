The ground under your tracks matters. Every map is a patchwork of surfaces, and each one changes how fast you pick up speed, how well you hold a slope and how easily you slide. Knowing what you are driving on helps you choose routes, keep your speed and avoid sliding into trouble.

## Grip and looseness

Two properties decide how a surface behaves.

**Grip** (traction) is how well your tracks or tyres bite into the ground. Good grip means you accelerate cleanly and are less likely to slide, whether you are turning hard or sitting on a hill. Where grip comes from depends on the ground:

- On soft ground, the grousers on a track or the tread of a tyre sink in and push against the soil.
- On hard ground that doesn't give, grip comes from plain friction between rubber or steel and the surface. Hard and wet is the worst case: tracks and wheels spin, acceleration suffers and skids become far more likely.

**Looseness** is how tightly packed the surface is. Firm ground resists your weight; loose ground lets you sink in, and very loose ground leaves you spinning in place.

Firmer is not always better, though. Once a track has dug in, what really matters is how well the material resists shearing, meaning how strongly its particles hold on to each other. Loose sand grains are hard and don't bind, so they just roll along with the track and much of its movement is wasted. Snow of the same density compacts and sticks together, so far less effort is wasted and you keep more speed.

Rules of thumb:

- More grip and firmer ground let you reach top speed sooner, hold it longer and slide less.
- Wider tracks have more contact with the ground, so they grip better.
- Wheeled vehicles grip worse than tracked ones.

## Roads and paved surfaces

- **Paved road:** the best surface in the game. Every vehicle performs at its best and skidding is almost ruled out. Mostly found in towns.
- **Dirt road:** a track packed down by traffic. Traction is good and it beats going cross-country, but you won't be as fast as on a paved road.
- **Sandy road:** rolled, compacted sand with good grip, much like a dirt road. Wheeled vehicles especially like it, and it does no harm to anything else. The rest of a sandy map is hard going, so use these roads to reach key positions before the enemy does.
- **Snow-covered road:** a buried road or just a well-used trail in fresh snow. You keep normal speed, but grip is slightly lower.
- **Paving stones:** common on city streets and pavements. Accelerating is easy, but the smooth stones give tracks little purchase when you brake hard, so you're more likely to slide.
- **Concrete:** rare. Like paving stones, it lets you reach normal speed but does little to stop a skid.

## Open ground

- **Normal dirt:** covers most maps and gives good grip and traction.
- **Mud:** fairly rare, usually inside bomb craters or around freshly dug trenches. It is thick and sticky, slows every vehicle badly, and leaves deep, obvious track marks behind you.
- **Rocks:** hard outcrops scattered over many maps. Grip is somewhat reduced, so drive carefully, but nothing bogs you down and speed builds normally.

## Sand and snow

- **Sand:** the main surface on desert maps, and one of the worst to drive on. It slows both driving and turning, and vehicles, wheeled ones above all, tend to skid and slide down steep slopes.
- **Sand dunes:** shaped by the wind and even looser than ordinary sand. Grip is worse still; expect a hard time.
- **Snow:** slow to accelerate on and slippery, so expect to slide about. Even so, you'd rather be on snow than sand.

## Ice

- **Ice:** rare, but the slipperiest thing on any map. Nothing grips worse or skids harder, and with tracks and tyres spinning, you pick up speed only slowly.
- **Icy rocks:** grip is worse than on ordinary rocks but better than on open ice, and speed is slightly lower than on ordinary rocks. It is easy to slip sideways off one and end up stuck.

## Water

Water isn't really a surface, but it matters just as much. Wading slows every land vehicle down a lot, amphibious ones too, and it makes no difference whether there's a road under the water or not. Don't go too deep, either, or the crew will drown.
