IRCM is an electronic jammer that protects an aircraft from heat-seeking missiles. Where a flare offers the missile a hotter target to chase, IRCM goes after the seeker itself: it either flickers infrared light at it to feed it false steering commands, or dazzles it with a laser so it can no longer see the aircraft at all.

## What it is fighting

An infrared seeker sees heat, nothing else. A jet engine glows against the cooler sky behind it, and the better missiles can even pick out an airframe warmed up by air friction. Seekers in the game come in two broad generations, and the difference matters for IRCM:

- **Spinning-detector seekers.** Older missiles use one detector that rotates. As it spins, it notes how far the hot spot sits from the centre of its view and steers to close that gap.
- **Imaging seekers.** Newer missiles use a grid of detectors that builds an actual picture of the target. Software then tracks the target's outline, and some can even work out what the target is.

A pulsing infrared light is enough to throw off the first kind. The second kind is far harder to fool.

## Where you will meet it

Most helicopters from rank 6 upwards carry some version of IRCM. It works best alongside [flares](/guide/mechanics/flares/): the jammer spoils the seeker while the flares give it something else to lock onto, which raises your odds of shaking the missile off. ## The three types

| System | How it works | Where the energy goes | Effective against |
|---|---|---|---|
| IRCM (omni-directional) | A strong infrared lamp pulses at a set frequency; a ring of spinning mirrors throws the light out in every direction around the aircraft | All round the aircraft | Older seekers |
| DIRCM | The same pulsing infrared light, but aimed straight at the incoming missile | At the missile, once the missile approach warning system detects it | Older seekers |
| LDIRCM (laser DIRCM) | An infrared laser pointed at the missile instead of a pulsing lamp | At the missile, triggered the same way as DIRCM | Older and imaging seekers |

### Omni-directional IRCM

The lamp's pulses create a phantom target in the seeker's view. An older missile reacts to it, wanders off its course and misses. The catch is that its energy is spread all round the aircraft instead of being concentrated on the threat.

### DIRCM

Directed IRCM waits for the missile warning system to spot a threat, then swings a beam of pulsing infrared light onto that missile. Squeezing the energy into one narrow beam makes it much stronger where it counts, so it works better than the all-round version.

### Laser DIRCM

This is the only one of the three that deals with modern image-based seekers. Rather than faking a target, the laser blinds the seeker outright. The missile can no longer find the aircraft and either self-destructs or flies off somewhere else.

## A real-world track record

During the Soviet war in Afghanistan, Mi-24 helicopters carried the SOEP-V1A "Lipa" IRCM set. Of 563 Stinger missiles fired at them, 89 hit, and only 31 of those hits did serious damage; most of the successful shots came from the system's blind spot. Across the whole war, 18 helicopters were lost to Stingers. The Su-25T, with its own IRCM, had just a 4.7% chance of being hit by one.
