Some tanks carry a dozer-style blade, either built in or bolted to the front, that lets them scrape up earth and dig themselves in. It arrived together with deformable terrain in Update 2.11 "Ground Breaking". With it you can build your own firing position: a mound of soil in front of the hull so that only the armoured turret looks over the top.

## Getting it ready

- **Research the modification.** The blade is a modification, so it has to be unlocked on the vehicle before you can use it.
- **Decide whether you want it fitted.** It adds weight, and you can take it off again if you would rather keep the vehicle lighter.
- **Bind the key.** The control lives under Controls → Tank → Miscellaneous → "Trenching: activate/deactivate". The default is Left Ctrl + T.

## Digging a position

1. Pick your spot before the enemy can see you. Digging slows the tank down, so doing it under fire is asking for trouble.
2. Lower the blade and drive forward. The blade pushes the soil ahead of you into a berm.
3. Back up and repeat. Two or three passes are usually enough to build useful cover.

The blade needs soft ground. It does nothing on asphalt or very hard surfaces, and it will not dig where buildings or rock block it. Open soil works well, and many maps have plenty of it: the Kursk steppe, the Normandy beaches, the Maginot Line fields, the sands of Sinai and the Aral Sea, and the snowy valleys of Finland and Volokolamsk.

## What the berm will and won't stop

Treat the earth as concealment with a bonus, not as armour. It adds a modest layer of protection and can soak up some lighter rounds and autocannon fire, but there is no guarantee it will stop a proper anti-tank shell. Its real value is hiding your weak spots so the enemy has nothing easy to aim at.

## Vehicles that benefit most

- **Strong turret, weak hull.** Many main battle tanks have a well-protected turret sitting on a much thinner hull. The T-72, T-80, Chieftain and Challenger are good examples. Dig in, or borrow a berm a teammate has already made, and the hull disappears from view.
- **ATGM carriers.** Vehicles such as the IT-1, UDES 33 and Wiesel 1A2 can sit behind the mound and show nothing but the missile launcher.
- **Tanks with poor gun depression.** Soviet designs are known for how little they can lower the gun. A short reverse to bring the gun out of the dug-in position can partly make up for it.

## The real equipment

In the Soviet Army, every main battle tank from the T-72 onwards, and some other armoured vehicles, came with a self-entrenching device, and digging in became a normal part of crew training. Many Western tanks also carry blades, either as standard or as part of engineering or urban-combat kits.

A typical blade has two settings. For travel it is folded up and held against the lower front plate by two clamps. For work it is dropped down on struts so its edge bites into the ground as the tank moves forward; when the tank reverses, the blade simply rides along on its back edge. Designs vary from heavy, semi-permanent welded fittings to quick-release bolt-on kits, but they all trade the same things against each other: protection, weight and how easy they are to fit and maintain.
