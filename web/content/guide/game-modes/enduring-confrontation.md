Enduring Confrontation (EC) is a separate aircraft mode built around long battles that you can drop into at any time. Respawns are unlimited within the mode's rules, and the outcome is shaped less by single dogfights than by what players do to the wider battle: escorting raids, tipping ground skirmishes, pushing the front line and wrecking airfields.

## Finding a battle

EC runs as an event, so you will find it under **Events and Tournaments** below the To Battle button. It is the main way to fly aircraft in Simulator Battles, and a simplified Arcade version is the only dedicated mode for helicopters.

You can either queue for it like a normal random battle, or open the list of rooms (ongoing battles, sorted by nation and rank) and join whichever side has a free slot in the room you want.

Compared with random battles, the balance of power in EC can swing back and forth several times as one side gains the upper hand, and the mode has mechanics found nowhere else, notably the front line and modular airfields.

## How to win

A team wins in one of two ways:

- **Shut down every enemy airfield.** Once every one of a team's airfields has lost both its airstrip and its living quarters, that team can no longer put new aircraft into the air, and it loses automatically.
- **Lead on points at the end.** Every battle has a time limit of 3 hours. When it runs out, the team with more victory points wins.

## Objectives

Victory points come from objectives of two kinds. **Explicit** objectives are listed on the map screen. **Hidden** objectives are always active but are not listed, simply to keep the list short. Both pay victory points.

### Always-on (hidden) objectives

- **Find enemy airfields** at the start of the battle, when nobody knows where they are yet.
- **Destroy an enemy airfield** in a given sector.
- **Help win a ground skirmish** in a sector. Skirmishes are fights between AI ground forces, and several can run at once, even in the same sector from different directions. An arrow on the map shows who is attacking: blue for your side, red for the enemy. The attacker fields tanks and howitzers; the defender has light pillboxes and howitzers.
- **Destroy enemy units.** Every enemy plane shot down costs its team 100 points, and every ground unit lost costs it 20.

### Listed (explicit) objectives

| Objective | What it involves |
|---|---|
| Spotter | A single AI aircraft flies in from its side's rear and circles over enemy territory, at an altitude that depends on the battle rank. While it is up, its team can discover objectives from further away. Cover yours, kill theirs. |
| Bombers or attack aircraft | An AI group of 6 bombers or 9 attack aircraft, escorted by 2 AI fighters. Bombers always go for the enemy base; attackers go for the most recent active ground skirmish. |
| Vehicle column | An AI convoy of tanks, trucks and mobile AA guns heading for the front. When it reaches its sector it raises its side's military presence there to the maximum, which starts a ground skirmish with the convoy's team attacking. Each team can have up to 3 active convoys. |
| Air supremacy | Capture an air sector next to the front line, marked by a small circle with an "A". Its area matches the map sector exactly, and its altitude is set at 6,000 m. Only player aircraft count, and time is limited. The winner gets a ground skirmish, as the attacker, in that sector or a neighbouring one. |
| Enemy base | Bomb the base in the indicated sector. How much punishment it takes depends on the battle rank. |
| Enemy carrier | On a few maps, such as Sicily and Tunisia, you first have to locate the enemy carrier and then sink it. It moves with an escort of destroyers, but only the carrier counts; hitting the escorts does nothing for the objective. |

### Discovering objectives

Some objectives only become active once found. Any friendly unit, AI or player, reveals one by getting close enough, although players see further than AI units, and a friendly spotter in the air extends the range for both. When an explicit objective is revealed, your whole team gets an on-screen message and it is added to the list on the map. If a player made the discovery, the team also earns victory points, usually around 20% of what completing the objective is worth.

## The map window

The map screen is your main tool in EC. It always shows:

- the mission, the location and the time left (top left);
- the vehicle set for the battle, with the cost of taking off in each aircraft, and your Silver Lions;
- your current aircraft setup, which you can change at an airfield before take-off: weapons, ammo belts, camouflage, convergence of forward guns, fuse delay and fuel;
- the list of active objectives;
- chat and the battle log;
- the mission map.

The map itself updates live with your position and heading, the front line, discovered airfields (your own are always shown; enemy ones must be found first), active bases and their condition, the air supremacy sector if there is one, active convoys, and active ground skirmishes with attack arrows and unit markers.

Each EC map is cut into a grid of equal squares, and the square size depends on the map: 8,192 m on the 64 x 64 km maps, 12,288 m on Dover, and 16,384 m on the largest maps such as Denmark, Sinai, Afghanistan, Spain and Vietnam.

## The front line

The front line follows the borders between map sectors and decides which side owns each one. It starts along the middle of the map and shifts as AI ground skirmishes are decided: a successful attack hands the sector to the attacker and moves the line. You influence this by destroying the AI ground units involved. Left alone, a skirmish is settled by the AI units damaging each other, and AI attack aircraft can also join in.

The line matters for airfields. If it moves so that an airfield ends up inside enemy territory, that airfield stops working, and it comes back into service if the line moves back.

## Airfields

All aircraft in EC take off from airfields and return to them to rearm and repair, which makes airfields the key strategic target in such long battles.

### Locations and detection

Whenever a new battle is generated, the server randomly chooses airfield locations for each side, with as many as five possible sites per side depending on the map. You always know where your own are; the enemy's must be found. You can spot one visually from altitude, but it only appears on the map once you fly within a few kilometres of it, whatever your height. AI units can discover airfields too.

At the top centre of the screen, above the victory point bar, circles show the state of discovered airfields. Blue circles are your airfields that the enemy has found; red circles are enemy airfields your team has found. How full each circle is shows the airfield's integrity, and no circles on a side means none of that side's airfields have been found yet.

### Defences

Each airfield is guarded by ten anti-aircraft units in three types: short, medium and long-range guns, plus surface-to-air missile units in high-tier battles (EC6 and up). Destroyed defences respawn after a while. Two fighters also circle overhead, attack anything that comes near and break off once the intruder gets far enough away. They respawn when shot down and sometimes land to refuel and rearm.

### Modules

Airfields are made of four separate zones, each with its own job, size, layout and durability:

| Module | Role |
|---|---|
| Airstrip | Where aircraft take off and land |
| Fuel depots | Fuel storage |
| Parking area | Aircraft parking and repairs |
| Living quarters | Where the crews are housed; also sets how fast damaged zones are repaired |

Zones do not overlap, and their durability rises with the rank of the EC mission. Only a bomb that lands inside a zone damages it; bombs that miss, or hit a zone already destroyed, are wasted. Damaged zones repair themselves over time, so an airfield can never be wiped out for good, only knocked out of action for a while. The one exception is the win condition above: wreck the airstrip and living quarters at all of a team's airfields and the battle ends.

## Building your set

Bring a mix of fighters, strike aircraft and bombers so you can handle enemy aircraft, help on the front line and raid bases and airfields. Pillboxes are best handled with rockets, while carriers and ground bases need bombs to be damaged at all. A fast climber with cannons is also worth including to catch AI attackers and bombers.
