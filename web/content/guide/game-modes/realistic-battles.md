Realistic sits between Arcade and Simulator. Vehicles perform about as close to their real counterparts as the game can make them, most of the hand-holding is gone, and every vehicle you spawn is worth more because you cannot simply throw it away and grab the next one. It is aimed at players who already know the basics, but it still keeps a few simplifications that Simulator drops.

## The three big changes

- **Real performance.** Speed, handling and weapon behaviour follow the real vehicles as closely as possible.
- **Limited respawns.** Depending on the branch, you either have to earn spawn points for each new vehicle or you get a single life per battle.
- **No shortcuts.** A destroyed module stops working, and ammunition is only replenished at capture points and airfields.

## Air battles

You fly one aircraft per battle: whatever you picked before the match is the only thing you get, with no respawn.

- **The airframe has limits.** Pulling too much G, or simply letting the airflow separate from the wings, can send you into a spin or break the plane apart.
- **Rearming means landing.** Guns, cannons, bombs and missiles are only refilled, and damage and fuel only fixed, by landing at a friendly airfield or carrier.
- **No lead marker.** You judge deflection yourself.
- **Spotting depends on your crew.** Whether an enemy gets a marker depends on where it is relative to the pilot's and gunners' line of sight, on clouds and sun glare, and on your crew's Keen Vision skill.

Flight and damage models are much less forgiving than in Arcade, so each aircraft's real strengths and weaknesses show clearly. Teams are often built around specific nations to echo historical match-ups, such as the USSR against Germany at Stalingrad or Japan against the US at Midway, and missions take their objectives from the battles they recreate, for example halting a German tank push at Kursk or fighting off a Japanese raid on Pearl Harbor.

## Ground battles

### How it feels

Tanks, aircraft and helicopters all share the same battlefield. Ground vehicles feel heavy: they pick up speed slowly, lose a lot of it in corners, slide less and respond in a jerkier way. Turning the turret and laying the gun take longer, and crew skill becomes obvious. A green gunner traverses first and only then slowly corrects elevation, while a trained one does both at once. Field repairs also take far longer than in Arcade, often up to 2 minutes.

Your vehicle counts as knocked out once it can no longer both move and shoot, which in practice means it needs at least 2 crew members left.

### What you can see

There is no coloured crosshair telling you where the shell will land or whether it will penetrate. Enemies have no nametags and are not tracked on the minimap. The only hint is a brief red icon on the minimap when a nearby teammate spots or hits an enemy, giving its rough position.

### Spawn points

Everyone starts with enough spawn points (SP) for a first vehicle and earns more through actions in battle. The price of a spawn depends on the vehicle type (light tanks usually cost less than heavies) and on how its battle rating compares with the highest BR in the match. As long as you have the SP and a vehicle that is still allowed out, you can respawn. A non-reserve vehicle can return one more time if you have a backup for it.

You can also spend SP on an aircraft or helicopter from your own lineup. Unlike in Ground Arcade there is no time limit on it, but Air Realistic rules such as limited fuel and ammunition apply.

In battles at BR 6.7 and higher, a nuclear-armed bomber is available for 2,500 SP. Dropping the bomb wins the round outright for your team and earns you the "Doomsday" award.

## Naval battles

- **Heavier ships.** Vessels take noticeably longer to accelerate and turn.
- **Harsher damage.** Some modules, such as ammunition elevators, are easier to damage, and losing them hurts the ship more.
- **Slower secondary weapons.** They take a little longer to deploy and, once empty, only refill at captured points, much like Ground Realistic.
- **Minimal markers.** Enemies you hit show on the minimap, but there are no tags with the ship's name or the player's name.

### Gunnery

Your gunners give you an initial range, but further corrections are up to you. A small "V" marker shows horizontal lead only, and it takes time to calculate and update. The range readout refreshes only now and then and carries a noticeable error. You can still adjust range by moving the mouse as in Arcade, but accurate fire needs the dedicated distance-correction controls, which change gun range independently of the mouse. Against aircraft, your gunners still estimate a firing point for you.

### Spawns and aircraft

Ship spawns follow the same rules as Naval Arcade. Aircraft behave as they do in Air Realistic: no rearming in flight, so once your payload is gone you have to land on an airfield or carrier. On maps where carriers are the only place to land, bigger planes can find this very hard.
