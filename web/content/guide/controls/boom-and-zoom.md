Boom and zoom (BnZ, sometimes called boom and run) is a way of fighting in which you never give the enemy a fair fight. You start with more energy than your target, usually by being higher, dive on it, fire as you pass, and use the speed from the dive to get away. Once you are clear, you look at the situation again and set up the next pass. What you never do is stay and turn with the enemy.

## Which aircraft suit it

Any plane can make a diving pass, but some are built for it. Good BnZ aircraft tend to have:

- heavy armament, so a short burst is enough;
- high straight-line speed;
- controls that do not lock up (compress) much at high speed, and a high wing-rip speed;
- a sturdy airframe;
- usually, a weaker climb rate and turn than the nimble fighters they face.

A large part of the American, German and Chinese trees leans this way. Some well-known examples:

| Aircraft | Why it works |
|---|---|
| P-47 Thunderbolt / P-43 Lancer | Lots of guns and very tough |
| P-38 Lightning | Climbs well, gets an air spawn, 20 mm cannon |
| Fw 190 series | Mixed gun and cannon armament for quick kills |
| Typhoon / Tempest | Britain's answer to the P-47; later versions are very well armed, and the Typhoon carries four Hispano cannons |
| P-51 Mustang | Dives fast, but compresses badly at high speed |
| F4U Corsair | Excellent dive speed and a deadly armament |
| F6F Hellcat | Not much of a dogfighter, but its guns are good enough for diving passes |
| I-185 | Good climb and nose-mounted cannons |
| Sea Fury | Huge speed and lethal cannons |
| Bf 109 series | Mainly energy fighters, but versions with cannon gunpods can boom and zoom |
| Ta 152 | The Fw 190's big brother, responsive at high speed |
| Do 335 A/B | Strong armament but sluggish to manoeuvre |
| J7W Shinden | Fast-firing 30 mm cannons; less agile than other Japanese aircraft |

Jets can also fight this way to a degree, as can heavy or twin-engined fighters that are fast enough and stay controllable at speed, such as the Firefly, SB2C and P-61.

## Step 1: get above them

Height is stored energy: the more you have over your target, the more speed you can turn it into during the attack. The catch is that many BnZ aircraft climb poorly compared with a Bf 109, La-5/7 or Spitfire. A bit of side-climbing, gaining height off to one side of the fight, helps.

A working altitude of 4,000 to 4,500 m (13,123 to 14,763 ft) is a good target, but you do not need to be the highest plane on the map. About 1 km (3,300 ft) of height over the enemy you are attacking is enough to fight well.

## Step 2: choose your target

- **Keep looking around.** Check where the enemies are every few seconds. It is very easy to fixate on one target and get jumped by another.
- **Highest enemy first, usually.** That is the common advice, but read the battle. Say you and one teammate are the last left on your side, you are at medium height, two enemies are chasing your teammate near the ground and a third is climbing towards you about 1,000 m below. Go and save your teammate, and take a swipe at the climber on the way down if you can.
- **Pick the straggler.** When enemies are bunched below you, go for the one at the back of the group, furthest from you. That way nobody in the group is behind you as you dive through.
- **Type matters less than awareness.** Diving passes work on agile fighters and slow bombers alike. An alert pilot in a nimble plane such as a Zero or Spitfire will try to dodge, though. If you miss, keep diving: those aircraft usually dive poorly and will not catch you.

## Step 3: the attack

1. **Set up.** Hold your altitude above the target and make sure it has not seen you.
2. **Dive.** Any angle works, but a steeper dive builds more speed, and that speed is what carries you out afterwards.
3. **Fire.** Choose a firing range you are comfortable with. Opening fire from too far away gives you away and buys the enemy time to react. A few solid hits per pass is plenty, and most targets will not see it coming.

## Step 4: get away

A common mistake is to pull straight back up into a steep climb after the pass. That burns the speed you just built and leaves you hanging in front of the enemy's guns as you slow down. Climbing away like this only suits aircraft that climb well, such as the J2M or Bf 109, or a pass where you are sure the target is dead.

Instead:

- carry on down for a moment, then flatten out into straight flight and let the speed from the dive pull you away;
- if the enemy does not follow, turn back gently (a hard turn throws your speed away) to attack again, or climb at a shallow angle;
- if the enemy does follow, keep running towards teammates, or, if you are willing to gamble, trade your speed for height and zoom up beyond the reach of their guns.

Once you are safe, regain some height and go again.

## When things do not go to plan

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| The target sees you and dodges | Keep diving until you are a bit lower, then use your speed to climb away at a shallow angle and line up another pass. |
| The target dives away as you come down | Either chase it, if your plane has a high rip speed and dives quickly, or pull out, keep your altitude and find another target, which is the better choice in a poor climber. Do not throw away your height just because they gave up theirs. |
| The target climbs up at you head-on | Open fire early to discourage the head-on. A climbing plane bleeds speed fast and will struggle to come round again, unless it is something with superb low-speed handling like a Zero. If its guns are better than yours and it keeps coming, pull out and climb back to safety. |
| The target tries to drag you into a climbing or energy fight | Fire a few shots and leave. Heavy BnZ aircraft cannot compete with better climbers in the vertical. |
| The target tries to out-turn you | Follow the turn briefly and take whatever good shots you get. Turn fighters at your own altitude are the biggest danger to a BnZ pilot, so remove them quickly. If you miss, dive away, then climb gently once you have extended. |
| You are the last one alive against several enemies | Attack the highest enemy. If a turn fighter offers an easy shot, take it before it comes back to haunt you. If you end up low, take head-on shots and finish fights fast, because anything more than one-on-one is hard in a heavy aircraft. |

The short version: do not get baited. Boom and zoom takes patience, but if you stick to your passes and keep your energy, it is a very effective way to fly. To find aircraft that suit this style, browse the [aircraft tech trees](/air/).
